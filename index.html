P4casting: Dual-Timescale Online Traffic Forecasting via Data–Control Plane Collaboration

IEEE HPCC 2026 (CCF-C, Accepted) · Second Author & Corresponding Author

github.com/f1ybaozii/p4casting

A dual-timescale online traffic prediction system combining hierarchical heavy/light-flow management and multidimensional feature collection in the data plane with cross-frequency and cross-variable attention in a control-plane deep learning model.

  • Real-time Traffic Forecasting
  • Programmable PISA Data Plane
  • Flow Management
Research details

Background & Motivation: Highly bursty, non-stationary encrypted network traffic makes accurate short-term prediction difficult and introduces latency challenges. P4casting addresses this problem with a dual-timescale online prediction system built through data-control plane synergy.

Design: The data plane combines Bloom filters, Count-Min Sketch, and exact flow tables for hierarchical heavy/light-flow management. It collects features across stateful/stateless and high-frequency/low-frequency dimensions. The control plane uses a dual-timescale deep learning model integrating cross-frequency attention and cross-variable attention for accurate online traffic prediction.

Results: The P4casting prototype was deployed on Intel Tofino 1 and evaluated on traffic prediction and burst detection tasks under multiple window configurations, outperforming several state-of-the-art time-series forecasting methods. Steady-state end-to-end latency was approximately 329.702 ms, demonstrating prediction accuracy and the feasibility of online deployment.

AURORA: An Adaptive Load Balancing Mechanism via Data Plane Prediction at Line Rate

IEEE ISCC 2026 (CCF-C, Accepted) · Third Author

A prediction-driven load balancing mechanism implemented entirely in the P4 data plane, combining integer-approximated Holt smoothing, adaptive server weights, and stateless connection consistency to respond to burst traffic at line rate.

  • Load Balancing
  • Programmable Switches
  • Traffic Prediction
Research details

Background & Motivation: Short-term bursts in data centers can cause delayed load balancing responses and server overload. AURORA uses traffic prediction on a P4 programmable data plane to provide adaptive scheduling at line rate without control-plane intervention.

Design: An integer approximation of Holt double-exponential smoothing predicts connection counts in the next time window. Traffic scaling coefficients dynamically adjust server weights, and probabilistic interval mapping distributes connections fairly. Server identifiers embedded in TCP timestamps preserve connection consistency in a stateless data plane, avoiding TCAM/SRAM flow-table overflow and supporting large numbers of concurrent connections.

Results: The prototype was deployed on Intel Tofino 1 in a single-leaf topology and evaluated with Poisson-arrival and Pareto-distributed traffic, including 10× and 20× burst windows. CPU utilization imbalance was reduced by 84.7%, flow completion time by 15.1%, and the TCP abnormal termination rate was 1.6%.

AdaTable: Learning to Calibrate for Robust VNF Auto-scaling under Capacity Drift

ICIC 2026 (Accepted) · Third Author

A dual-plane VNF auto-scaling framework that learns capacity-table calibration to balance resource cost and SLA compliance under hidden capacity drift.

  • Network Function Virtualization
  • VNF Auto-scaling
  • Constrained Reinforcement Learning
Research details

Motivation: Changing effective service capacity makes traffic-based scaling unreliable.

Design: A deterministic fast plane executes an anti-jitter capacity table; a DRL slow plane calibrates its parameters using adaptive penalties.

Results: Near the 5% SLA target, average active instances decrease by about 31% versus HPA, with SLA violations falling from 7.77% to 3.94%.

RecPipe: Top-Down Distillation of RNN Models for Data-Plane Traffic Classification

USENIX NSDI 2027 (CCF-A, Under Review) · First Author

A two-stage framework that distills per-flow RNN models into finite-state representations and deploys recurrent inference through lookup tables on programmable switches, making use of flow history within hardware resource budgets.

  • RNN
  • Finite-State Machines
  • Traffic Classification
  • Programmable Data Plane
  • Intel Tofino 1
Research details

Background & Motivation: Data-plane traffic classification faces difficulties in deploying neural models, model-structure constraints imposed by switch table resources, and inefficient use of flow history. RecPipe addresses these challenges with a two-stage RNN deployment method that converts recurrent inference into a finite-state representation.

Design: The offline stage first trains an RNN on a per-flow basis, then constructs discrete codebooks for hidden states and inputs, searches for an optimal finite-state machine within the hardware budget, and prunes deployment table entries. The online stage encodes per-packet features and performs multi-step state-transition inference through pipeline table lookups. Mirrored packets commit hidden states, enabling RNN inference across pipeline traversals.

Results: RecPipe was evaluated on Intel Tofino 1 using five public datasets. It achieved a Macro-F1 improvement of 2.25–7.30% on three datasets. In concurrent-flow-capacity experiments at 100 Gbps, classification performance remained stable as flow capacity increased and outperformed the comparison methods at the highest tested capacity.

Granulaxis: Evidence-Driven Granularity Refinement for Attack Mitigation in Programmable Networks

IEEE INFOCOM 2027 (CCF-A, Under Review) · Fifth Author

An evidence-driven switch–DPU–controller framework that chooses mitigation granularity online, balancing attack coverage, collateral damage, and limited rule capacity through temporary DPU statelets and budget-aware admission.

  • Attack Mitigation
  • Data Processing Units (DPUs)
  • Mitigation Granularity
  • Programmable Switches
  • Resource Allocation
Research details

Background & Motivation: Programmable switches can mitigate attacks at line rate, but finite table capacity creates a tradeoff: aggregate actions conserve rules while risking collateral damage, whereas selective child actions require more entries. Granulaxis decides when the available evidence justifies refinement.

Design: Coarse switch signals trigger short-lived evidence leases. During each lease, a DPU statelet summarizes the attack-relevant child structure of a suspicious traffic aggregate. The controller first chooses a single parent action or an adaptive heavy-child action for each object, then admits selected actions across objects under a shared rule budget. Enforcement continues while evidence is collected off the forwarding path.

Results: The prototype combines Intel Tofino and NVIDIA BlueField-3. On concentrated SYN and DNS workloads it removes over 99.9% of attack traffic, with no observed benign loss across five seeds. Under a 32-entry budget, it improves attack reduction by 7.0 percentage points and reduces benign loss by 3.3 percentage points relative to Evidence-First.
